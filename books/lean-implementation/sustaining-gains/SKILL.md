# Sustaining the Gains (巩固成果)

## R — Reading

> "If progress is your desire, then you need to fix it right, and fix it only once." — Ch 10, p.164

## I — Interpretation

Sustaining gains separates companies that prosper from those that don't. Two techniques:

**1. Maintenance (维护)**
- Ability to restore equipment to original condition
- TPM (Total Productive Maintenance) as the systematic approach
- Operators participate in maintenance

**2. Standardization (标准化)**
- Ability to get all people, machines, and methods to continue doing what has been shown to be effective
- Document the best known method
- Audit to ensure compliance

**Why gains are lost:**
- "It is simply a lot sexier to move on to the next problem and solve it"
- Natural entropy — all systems degrade without maintenance
- Employee turnover — the most difficult type of change
- Customer demands increase over time
- Internal improvement efforts create change that must be managed

**Key enemies of sustaining:**
- Solving the same problem repeatedly without institutionalizing the fix
- Not documenting improvements
- Not auditing compliance
- Employee turnover without knowledge transfer

## A1 — Past Application

The author's company (Quality Consultants) specializes in sustaining gains. Their motto: "The only effective approach to quality is to make it part of your culture." Companies that don't institutionalize improvements spend time solving the same problems repeatedly.

## A2 — Future Trigger

- "We fixed this problem before but it came back"
- "How to make Lean improvements permanent?"
- "Our gains keep reverting after the consultant leaves"
- "How to prevent backsliding?"
- "What's the difference between companies that sustain Lean and those that don't?"

## E — Execution

1. **Document the improvement**: Standard work must capture the new method
2. **Train everyone**: All operators must know the new standard
3. **Audit regularly**: Check compliance to standard work
4. **Address deviations immediately**: Don't let small slips accumulate
5. **Build maintenance systems**: TPM for equipment, standard work for processes
6. **Transfer knowledge**: Document everything so turnover doesn't destroy gains
7. **Expand carefully**: Don't expand to new areas until current areas are sustained

## B — Boundary

- **NOT for**: Situations where the improvement hasn't been proven yet
- **NOT for**: Quick-fix solutions that haven't been validated
- **Requires**: Discipline to audit and enforce standards
- **Warning**: Employee turnover is the biggest threat — document everything
- **Limitation**: Sustaining is harder than improving — requires ongoing effort
