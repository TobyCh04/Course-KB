# Candidate Cases — How to Implement Lean Manufacturing

## C1: The Alpha Line (Ch 14)
- **Company**: Unnamed manufacturer (author's consulting client)
- **Industry**: Discrete manufacturing
- **Methodology applied**: Full Lean implementation using the Eight-Step Prescription
- **What happened**: Complex production line converted from flow line to cellular manufacturing. Production rate improved by 63% with virtually no capital expenditures and less manpower.
- **Lesson**: Cellular manufacturing with right-sized equipment and cross-trained operators can dramatically improve throughput without capital investment.

## C2: The Bravo Line (Ch 15)
- **Company**: Unnamed manufacturer
- **Industry**: Discrete manufacturing
- **Methodology applied**: Lead time reduction through Lean implementation
- **What happened**: Reduced lead times significantly, achieved early gains in flexibility and responsiveness. Demonstrated how lead time reduction enables faster plan changes and better delivery performance.
- **Lesson**: Lead time reduction is the key enabler of manufacturing flexibility and responsiveness.

## C3: ABC Widgets Co. (Ch 17)
- **Company**: ABC Widgets Co. (Maquiladora in Mexico)
- **Industry**: Discrete manufacturing
- **Methodology applied**: Lean implementation (but poorly executed)
- **What happened**: On-time delivery dropped to 74% and stayed there for 5 months. Daily demand increased from 30 to 36 units/day. Customer switched to daily pickups. Home office sent 22 projects but plant couldn't improve. After 4 years of Lean initiative, basic foundational problems still persisted.
- **Lesson**: "How Not to Implement Lean" — wanting to LOOK Lean rather than BE Lean. Internal conflicts, too few onsite resources, large resistance to change. Lean skills were centrally located at home office, not at the plant.

## C4: Welding Machine Bottleneck (Ch 12/17)
- **Company**: ABC Widgets Co.
- **Industry**: Manufacturing
- **Methodology applied**: Constraint management, bottleneck analysis
- **What happened**: Reliability manager identified welding machine with low availability as major contributor to on-time delivery problem. Used constraint management to address the bottleneck.
- **Lesson**: Bottleneck reduction provides "low-hanging fruit" for early process gains. Often the constraint is not where you initially think.

## C5: Kanban Calculation Example (Ch 3)
- **Company**: Unnamed (author's consulting examples)
- **Industry**: Various manufacturing
- **Methodology applied**: Kanban calculations for pull system design
- **What happened**: Showed how to calculate kanban sizes, finished goods inventory levels, safety stock, and buffer stock using demand data, lead times, and variation factors.
- **Lesson**: Kanban sizing is a quantitative exercise, not a guess. Proper calculations prevent both overstocking and stockouts.

## C6: Value Stream Mapping Application (Ch 7)
- **Company**: Unnamed (author's consulting examples)
- **Industry**: Various manufacturing
- **Methodology applied**: Value stream mapping (VSM) per Rother and Shook's "Learning to See"
- **What happened**: Created present state and future state value stream maps to identify waste and plan improvements.
- **Lesson**: VSM makes the invisible visible — shows where time is actually spent (95-99% non-value-added).

## C7: Three Case Studies (Ch 16)
- **Company**: Three unnamed companies
- **Industry**: Various manufacturing
- **Methodology applied**: The Prescription from Ch 8 applied to three different facilities
- **What happened**: Each company applied the same prescription but with different results based on their commitment level and precursor readiness.
- **Lesson**: The prescription works, but results depend heavily on management commitment and precursor readiness.

## C8: Ohno's Lead Time Insight (Ch 5)
- **Company**: Toyota
- **Industry**: Automotive manufacturing
- **Methodology applied**: Lead time reduction as core strategy
- **What happened**: Ohno quoted: "All we are doing is looking at the time line, from the moment the customer gives us an order to the point when we collect the cash… And we are reducing that time line by removing the non-value-added wastes."
- **Lesson**: For Toyota, lead time reduction is the primary method for improving cash flow and manufacturing performance.

## C9: Cell Redesign Calculation (Ch 13)
- **Company**: Unnamed (author's consulting example)
- **Industry**: Discrete manufacturing
- **Methodology applied**: Cellular manufacturing redesign with production rate improvement calculations
- **What happened**: Redesigned complex production line from long flow line into cellular manufacturing, improving production rate by 63% with virtually no capital expenditures and less manpower.
