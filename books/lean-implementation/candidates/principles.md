# Candidate Principles — How to Implement Lean Manufacturing

## P1: Culture Before Tools (Ch 6)
- **Statement**: Change the culture BEFORE applying Lean tools. Without cultural change, tools will not sustain.
- **Context**: Any Lean implementation initiative
- **Consequence**: Tools are applied but gains are lost within months; workers revert to old habits
- **Quote**: "Leadership, motivation, and problem solving are the three fundamental issues in any cultural change."

## P2: One Value Stream at a Time (Ch 8)
- **Statement**: Implement Lean one value stream at a time, not as a "clean sweep" of the entire facility.
- **Context**: Large, complex manufacturing plants with multiple value streams
- **Consequence**: Overwhelming resources, diluted focus, failure to sustain gains
- **Quote**: "To install a Lean manufacturing system for all value streams in a large complex plant simultaneously is very difficult."

## P3: Protect the Customer First (Ch 8)
- **Statement**: Goal #1 in any Lean implementation is to protect the supply to the customer.
- **Context**: Before implementing any changes that could disrupt production
- **Consequence**: Customer loses confidence, orders lost, business damaged
- **Application**: Implement finished goods inventory controls before other kaizen activities

## P4: Fix It Right, Fix It Once (Ch 10)
- **Statement**: If you're going to fix a problem, fix it properly and permanently. Don't move on to the next problem until the current one is institutionalized.
- **Context**: Sustaining Lean gains
- **Consequence**: Problems reappear, same problems solved repeatedly, wasted effort
- **Quote**: "If progress is your desire, then you need to fix it right, and fix it only once."

## P5: Standardize Before Improving (Ch 10)
- **Statement**: Standardize the current process before trying to improve it. You can't improve what isn't stable.
- **Context**: Process improvement initiatives
- **Consequence**: Improvements built on unstable processes don't sustain; chaos increases

## P6: Multiskilled Workers Are Essential (Ch 4)
- **Statement**: Workers must be cross-trained on multiple operations. This is non-negotiable for Lean.
- **Context**: Staffing production cells, implementing flow
- **Consequence**: Cells can't flex with demand changes; work redistribution is impossible; Lean dynamics are lost
- **Quote**: "If the workers are not multiskilled, the dynamics of Lean are lost."

## P7: Problem Solving by All (Ch 4)
- **Statement**: Everyone must solve problems, not just engineers. Workers solve simple problems; escalation happens on a time trigger.
- **Context**: Daily production operations
- **Consequence**: Problems accumulate, workers feel powerless, system degrades
- **Key concept**: In TPS, problems are viewed as a weakness in the system, not a personal failure

## P8: Line Shutdown on Abnormality (Ch 4)
- **Statement**: Any worker can shut down the production line when they detect an abnormality.
- **Context**: Production operations with jidoka implementation
- **Consequence**: If workers can't stop the line, defects propagate and quality problems multiply
- **Cultural requirement**: Problems must be seen as system weakness, not worker failure

## P9: Inventory Hides Problems (Ch 3)
- **Statement**: Inventory acts as a buffer that hides underlying problems (quality issues, machine breakdowns, supplier delays). Reduce inventory to expose problems.
- **Context**: When evaluating why inventory exists
- **Consequence**: Problems remain hidden and unsolved; continuous improvement is blocked

## P10: Lead Time Is the Key Metric (Ch 5)
- **Statement**: Lead time is the primary measure of "Leanness." If you can reduce lead time, everything else improves.
- **Context**: Measuring Lean progress
- **Consequence**: Focusing on cost alone misses the point; lead time reduction drives cost reduction, quality improvement, and flexibility

## P11: Takt Time Sets the Pace (Ch 7)
- **Statement**: All production must be synchronized to takt time (available production time / customer demand rate).
- **Context**: Production planning and line balancing
- **Consequence**: Without takt, overproduction or underproduction occurs; inventory grows

## P12: Right-Size Equipment (Ch 13)
- **Statement**: Equipment in cells should be right-sized for the cell's needs, not the largest available machine.
- **Context**: Cell design and equipment selection
- **Consequence**: Oversized equipment creates batch-oriented thinking, increases space, reduces flexibility

## P13: Five Precursors Must Be in Place (Ch 19)
- **Statement**: Before starting Lean, ensure: (1) Management commitment, (2) Stable processes, (3) Reliable suppliers, (4) Adequate workforce training, (5) Proper planning model (MRP managed correctly)
- **Context**: Pre-implementation assessment
- **Consequence**: Skipping precursors leads to Lean initiative failure
- **Quote**: "The five precursors to Lean are not properly addressed!" (Reason #10 for Lean failure)

## P14: Sustain Before Expanding (Ch 10)
- **Statement**: Don't expand Lean to new areas until gains in current areas are fully sustained and institutionalized.
- **Context**: Multi-phase Lean rollout
- **Consequence**: Backsliding in completed areas; credibility loss; wasted effort

## P15: Measure and Post Flexibility/Responsiveness (Ch 5)
- **Statement**: Manufacturing flexibility and responsiveness should be measured and posted alongside other business metrics.
- **Context**: Performance measurement systems
- **Consequence**: Without measurement, flexibility/responsiveness improvements can't be tracked or valued
